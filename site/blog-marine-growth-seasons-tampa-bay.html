Why Marine Growth Explodes in Summer in Tampa Bay

Quick answer. Marine growth in Tampa Bay follows the seasons. Barnacle, oyster and algae growth picks up as the water warms in late spring and is fastest in summer, then slows in fall, with algae and mold still showing up on shaded docks in winter. Most homes do well with cleanings in late spring and fall.

Waterfront owners often notice that a dock cleaned in spring looks rough again by late summer. That is not your imagination. Marine growth in Tampa Bay follows the seasons.

Warm water means fast growth

As Gulf and bay water warms in late spring, barnacle and oyster larvae become more active and settle on hard surfaces. Algae grows faster with more sunlight and heat. Summer is peak season.

What each season looks like

  • Spring: growth picks up as water warms. A great time to clean before peak season.
  • Summer: fastest barnacle, oyster and algae growth of the year.
  • Fall: growth slows. A good time for a cleanup after summer and storm season.
  • Winter: slowest growth, but algae and mold still build on shaded docks.

How to time your cleanings

  • Twice a year: late spring and fall, which works for most homes.
  • Once a year: late spring, before hurricane season.
  • Heavy growth spots: on a plan with more frequent visits.

Location matters too

Canal homes and shaded docks grow faster than open bayfront docks with strong tidal flow. We factor that in when recommending a schedule.
