Seawall Maintenance for Canal Communities and HOAs

Quick answer. Slow canal water in communities such as Apollo Beach, Ruskin, Shore Acres and Bradenton grows oysters, barnacles and algae quickly. An HOA plan can put seawall faces, caps and common docks on one schedule, with photos the board can use when planning repairs. Individual owners can add lifts and ladders.

By Shea Ganison, Owner, Sea Dog Marine · Serving Pinellas, Hillsborough and Manatee Counties · 2 min read

Canal communities are some of the best places to live on the water in Tampa Bay, and some of the hardest on seawalls. Lower water flow lets oysters and barnacles build up quickly, and one neglected wall can affect neighbors.

Why canals grow so much

Dead end and slow moving canals keep warm, nutrient rich water in place. That is ideal for oysters, barnacles and algae. Communities in Apollo Beach, Ruskin, Shore Acres, Snell Isle, Holmes Beach and Bradenton all see heavy growth.

Benefits of a community approach

  • Consistent appearance along the canal.
  • Group scheduling that saves time and cost for every owner.
  • A shared photo record of seawall condition, helpful for planning repairs.

What an HOA plan can include

  • Seawall face and cap cleaning on a set schedule.
  • Common dock, boat ramp and kayak launch cleaning.
  • Optional add ons for individual owners such as lifts and ladders.
  • Pre storm and post storm photo documentation.
