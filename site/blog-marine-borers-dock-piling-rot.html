Marine Borers and Piling Rot: What Tampa Bay Homeowners Should Know

Quick answer. Marine borers are small organisms that tunnel into submerged wood and weaken dock pilings, mostly in the tidal zone and just below the low tide line. Thinning, soft spots, small holes or a piling that flexes are warning signs, and barnacles and oysters hide them. Cleaning the tidal zone and wrapping sound pilings helps protect the dock.

By Shea Ganison, Owner, Sea Dog Marine · Serving Pinellas, Hillsborough and Manatee Counties · 2 min read

Some of the most serious damage to a dock happens where you cannot easily see it. Wood pilings in salt and brackish water are attacked by marine borers and decay, mostly in the tidal zone and just below it.

What are marine borers?

Marine borers are small organisms that tunnel into submerged wood. Over time they hollow out pilings from the inside or erode them from the outside, reducing their strength even when the surface looks fine.

Where damage happens

  • In the tidal zone, where wood is wet and dry each day.
  • Just below the low tide line.
  • Around bolts and cuts where treated wood is exposed.

Warning signs

  • Pilings that look thinner, hourglass shaped or eroded near the waterline.
  • Soft spots when probed.
  • Small holes or channels in the surface.
  • Pilings that move or flex when the dock is loaded.

Why growth makes it worse

Heavy barnacle and oyster crust hides all of these signs. You cannot judge a piling you cannot see.

How to protect your pilings

  • Clean pilings through the tidal zone so you can see their condition.
  • Wrap sound pilings to create a barrier against borers.
  • Repair or replace badly damaged pilings before they fail under load.
