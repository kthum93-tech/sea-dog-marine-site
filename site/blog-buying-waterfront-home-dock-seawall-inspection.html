Buying a Waterfront Home? What to Check on the Dock and Seawall

Quick answer. Buying a waterfront home in Pinellas, Hillsborough or Manatee county includes the dock, seawall and often a boat lift, and those structures can be expensive to repair. Check for cracks, leaning, soil loss, soft boards, thin pilings and worn lift cables. Barnacles and oysters can hide that damage, so a cleaning during the inspection period shows the true surface.

When you buy a waterfront home in Pinellas, Hillsborough or Manatee county, you are also buying a dock, a seawall and maybe a boat lift. Those structures can be expensive to repair, so it pays to look closely before closing.

Seawall checklist

  • Cracks or spalling on the face and cap.
  • Leaning or bowing.
  • Soil loss or sinkholes behind the wall.
  • Open joints between panels.

Dock checklist

  • Soft, cracked or loose boards.
  • Pilings that look thin or eroded at the waterline.
  • Rusted or missing hardware.

Boat lift checklist

  • Cable and drum condition.
  • Motor and switch operation.
  • Cradles, bunks and beams.

The problem with growth

Heavy barnacle and oyster growth can hide much of what you need to see. An inspector looking at a crusted seawall can only guess at the concrete underneath.

How a cleaning helps buyers

With the seller's permission, a professional cleaning before or during the inspection period exposes the true surface of the wall and pilings. You get clear photos to share with your inspector, your contractor and your insurer.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I check on a seawall before buying?

Look for cracks, leaning, soil loss behind the wall, open joints and rust stains.

Can marine growth hide seawall damage?

Yes. Barnacles and oysters can cover cracks and spalling, which cleaning exposes.
